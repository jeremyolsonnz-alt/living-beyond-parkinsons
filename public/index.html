What this is

Not recovery. Not only acceptance.

Parkinson's is a progressive condition, so the language of "recovery" — of getting back to how things were — doesn't belong here, and this site will never use it. But it would be too cautious to offer only comfort and acceptance, because for some Parkinson's symptoms there is real, published evidence that mental practice can help — modestly, as a complement to medical care, but genuinely.

Where this site stands

Symptom management and quality of life, within a progressive condition — not getting better in the sense of going back, but living well, and sometimes moving with a little more ease.

What you can count on

The promises this site keeps

Free, and staying that way

No paywall, no email required, no ads. Everything here is open, and it will stay open.

Honest about the evidence

Every claim is tied to a named study and badged by strength. Weak evidence is never dressed up as strong.

A complement, never a replacement

These practices sit alongside medication, physiotherapy, and your Parkinson's team — never in place of any of them.

If anything here ever seems to be standing in for your medical care, that's a signal to step back, not lean in. The purpose of this site is to sit quietly beside good treatment.

Who made this

Built by Jeremy Olson

Jeremy Olson is a psychology graduate and solution-oriented hypnosis practitioner based in Auckland, New Zealand, with a background in quality-of-life research. He does not have Parkinson's himself, and doesn't claim the authority of lived experience — this site grows out of his research training and his work in the Ericksonian therapeutic tradition, offered in good faith and openly labelled as a personal synthesis.
