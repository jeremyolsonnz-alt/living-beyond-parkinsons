The Guided Imagery Programme

Five practices for living well with Parkinson's, each built on the same calm, four-part structure. You can begin with whichever one speaks to your day — there's no order to follow and nothing to complete.

How each practice is shaped

A four-part structure

Every practice moves through the same four phases. The shape is meant to be gentle and familiar, so that once you've done one, you know your way around all of them.

Opening

Breath and Light

Settling and arriving — an unhurried start that lets the body ease.

Phase I

The Ideal Being

A figure of easeful movement is watched, then steps into you — so watching becomes doing.

Phase II

The Pathway

A gentle picture of the body's own workings for that movement or state.

Closing

Coming Back

A soft return, carrying a little of the ease with you — nothing owed, nothing to prove.

The figure, and one deliberate exception

The heart of each motor practice is Phase I: a figure appears at a distance, is watched moving with ease, and then "steps into you," so that the feeling of easeful movement is borrowed from the inside. The figure is never a pre-Parkinson's self to chase back — there's no going back, and the practices never imply it. It's simply an embodiment of ease, offered to the body as it is now.

The one exception is Calm Beneath the Storm, the non-motor practice. Calm isn't an action to rehearse, and asking you to "become" a serene figure could carry an unwanted message that where you are now is wrong. So that practice sets the figure aside and simply arrives at a settled place directly — a restorative exception, made on purpose.

Two things to know before you begin

Honesty about the method

On the recordings

A synthesised version of a real voice

The guided audio is not recorded yet. When it is, the narration will use an ElevenLabs voice clone of Jeremy's own voice — a synthesised version of a real person's voice. That will be disclosed on every practice page, so you always know what you're listening to. Until then, each practice shows its full script to read instead.

This four-part structure is Jeremy's own synthesis. It draws on the Ericksonian and solution-oriented tradition he trained in, and its individual ingredients — motor imagery, action observation, relaxation and breath work — each have research behind them, gathered in the bibliography. But the combined protocol has not been tested as a unit in any clinical trial, and every practice says so. It's offered in good faith as a considered personal design, not as a proven treatment — and always as a complement to medication, physiotherapy, and your Parkinson's team, never a replacement.

The five practices

Choose where to begin

Each carries an honest evidence badge. One is Moderate — the research is real but heavily qualified; four are Emerging — early, indirect, or, in the case of overall motor symptoms, contradicted by the most recent meta-analysis. The badge describes the science, not how helpful you might personally find the practice.

Practice 1Moderate

Walking Freely

Gait & freezing of gait

Rehearsing the smooth approach and the way through a doorway, for the step that catches.
Practice 2Emerging

Finding Flow

Movement initiation & bradykinesia

For the pause before movement and the shrinking of it — rehearsing an easy, full-sized start.
Practice 3Emerging

Steady Ground

Balance & postural stability

A gentle, seated rehearsal of calm, unhurried steadiness — a quiet companion to physiotherapy.
Practice 4Emerging

The Steady Hand

Fine motor & handwriting

For writing that shrinks and fingers that fumble — rehearsing a hand that stays full and fluid.
Practice 5Emerging

Calm Beneath the Storm

Non-motor wellbeing — anxiety, "off"-period distress, sleep

A settling practice for the hard hours — meeting the storm differently, not making it disappear.
