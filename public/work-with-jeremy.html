About Jeremy Olson

A researcher's background, a therapist's tradition

Jeremy Olson is based in Auckland, New Zealand. His work sits at the meeting point of two things: a research training in what makes for a good quality of life, and a practitioner's grounding in the gentle, solution-oriented therapeutic tradition — the same tradition that shapes the guided imagery on this site.

Being straight with you

What Jeremy is, and isn't

A transparency note

Jeremy does not have Parkinson's

He also has no lived experience of spinal cord injury or stroke, the subjects of the companion sites. He isn't speaking as someone who has been through this condition, and he doesn't claim the particular authority that comes from that.

What he brings instead is a background in quality-of-life research and years of work in the Ericksonian, solution-oriented tradition — brought together here into a set of practices offered in good faith, and openly labelled throughout as a personal synthesis rather than a tested clinical protocol. The research and bibliography pages lay out exactly what the evidence does and doesn't support.

Working together

If you'd like to go further

The practices on this site are free and self-guided, and always will be — you never need to work with Jeremy to use any of them. For some people, a bit of company or conversation alongside them is worth having too.

Lead offering

The Practice Circle

KohaGive what feels right

A small, recurring group that meets over Zoom, open to anyone anywhere. One unhurried hour: a guided practice done live together, followed by conversation. Cameras on or off, no obligation to speak, and no one is ever put on the spot. The sense of company is part of what makes it worthwhile — not just the practice itself.

"Koha is a Māori custom of voluntary giving: you give what feels right to you, in response to what you've received."

There's no set price, no suggested amount, and no expectation either way. Giving nothing is genuinely welcome — "no one will ever know the difference."

When something is more personal

One to one

KohaGive what feels right

Some things are easier to talk about one to one than in a group. If there's something you'd rather raise privately, a one-to-one conversation in the same solution-oriented tradition is available — just the two of you. This might include things like:

  • Bladder or bowel changes (autonomic symptoms)
  • Constipation
  • Sexual difficulties
  • Swallowing
  • Speech
  • Sleep disturbance

What this is, and isn't. Both of these are personal support, offered in the solution-oriented tradition — not medical treatment, and not a promise of recovery. Parkinson's is progressive; nothing here reverses that. The aim is more ease, calm, and a sense of agency alongside the condition as it is, not a return to how things were before.
